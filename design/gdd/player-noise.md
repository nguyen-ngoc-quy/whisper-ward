## Overview

The **Player Noise (`NoiseEmitter`)** system is the facility's hearing contract — a thin, **ledger-free, event-driven relay with bounded session state** that turns the Approved Player Controller's `step_event` stream into broadcast, attributable raw `NoisePublished` facts, and the player's primary manipulation verb for *You Create the Situation*. In the MVP it owns two sources: **movement pips** (one raw fact per controller stride-commit, at the latched `stride_target_state` radius — walk `R_walk 4.0 m`, run `R_run 6.0 m`) and the **throwable Burst tool** (a **limited-resource** pickup that lands as an `R_burst 10.5 m` point-source). The emitter **owns no ledger**: the global audible-distance ledger `L_audible`, stride thresholds, remainder, freeze-without-reset, and `audible_armed` all live in Controller F5 — the emitter consumes `step_event`s and publishes facts, recomputing nothing. Its only state is bounded deduplication for the current `(session_id, attempt_epoch)`, the emitter `fact_id` allocator, and one Burst `flight_handle_id`; all are reset by the session transition barrier, never by pooling. Each raw fact carries kind, source position, radius, attribution (`movement / Burst`), and an emitter-allocated transport **`fact_id`** on the provisional Event bus; the Approved Perception system (R5) creates a **broadcast per-guard `NoiseHeardRelay`** (each guard relays independently, one guard's consumption never invalidates another's), gated by a wall-blocking `Physics.Linecast` from a `f12_noise_origin_offset 0.25 m`-raised origin to the guard's eye, and the Approved Guard AI FSM consumes the relay as an Investigate/orientation trigger — **never accumulator charge, never Chase on noise alone**. Without it there is no way to bait or divert a guard, no bounded consequence for fruitless searches, and no *You Create the Situation* decision; with it, a player who reads a patrol can *reshape* the room. It sits directly on the Player Controller, Physics, Event bus, and Input; it is consumed by Perception, Guard AI, and Level acoustic authoring. Concrete AudioSource pooling, mixer routing, and trigger-layer pins become an ADR at Technical Setup — the GDD stays at behavior.

*Player fantasy in one line:* you are the quiet architect who chooses when to be heard — a silent crouch that buys safety, a walk that risks a whisper, a run that trades speed for sound, and a Burst you carry like a single bullet and place to pull a guard off his line and slip the gap you made.

## Player Fantasy

**You are the quiet architect — you choose when the facility hears you, and every sound you let it hear reshapes the room.**

This is a **direct** system: the player actively decides to be silent or to be loud, and the world answers visibly. The core emotion is *controlled risk* — the held-breath tension of moving silently and the deliberate release of throwing sound where you want attention to go. It serves **Pillar 3: You Create the Situation** first — Burst is the verb that lets a planner pull a guard off his line and slip the gap they made — and **Pillar 1: Thinking Enemies** second, because the guard's head-turn, orient, and investigate is the proof the machine was listening. *(MVP Pillar 1 = the single-guard orient tell; multi-guard escalation — peer-recruit/locked-zone — is Target-tier, gated on Alert Propagation.)* **Pillar 2: Fair Mind-Challenge** is the guardrail: a detection after a noise is always readable as "I was too loud, too close, or too exposed after I called him," never as a random hearing cone.

**The anchoring moment:** you crouch-still behind sub-1.0 m cover, a guard's cone sweeps past, you walk three steps — the controller's stride ledger banks the distance; you sprint to a corner — the stride ledger commits, a `Noise heard` relay fires, the guard's head snaps and he walks to the sound's edge, not to you; you place a Burst on the far wall, hear the raised-origin Linecast clear, watch both in-range guards turn, and thread behind them while they investigate the *area with error* (never your exact position). The fantasy is not "I have a noisemaker" — it is "I made the patrol *wrong* and the facility proved it."

**What makes it sing vs. what kills it:**
- *Sings* when silence is a choice with cost (crouch is silent but slow — `V_crouch 1.8 < V_patrol 2.3`, so silence trades time), when walk/run risk is readable before you commit (stride thresholds 1.9 m / 2.6 m give ~0.55 s / ~0.46 s fresh-session windows to the first pip, and a **player-local rustle pre-warns the imminent commit**), and when a Burst feels like an authored placement, not a grenade spam (one precise throw, one investigation arc, bounded residual, a **limited resource you must reclaim by routing to a pickup**, not a free distraction).
- *Dies* if noise is noisy UI (a persistent "you are loud" glow), if hearing is a hidden radius with no orient tell, or if fruitless noise has no consequence (then Burst is a free win button — Pillar 3's anti-puppeting fails).

**Tone:** Cold Watch — sound is light's twin. A heard noise is the building exhaling: a head turn, a footstep toward the source, a torch sweep of an area. No horror stinger, no combat bark — surgical, institutional, visible intelligence.

## Detailed Rules

### Core Rules

1. **Single `NoiseEmitter` on the player, two emission channels.** The component has exactly two sources in the **MVP profile**, never more: **(a) Movement** — a stateless relay that publishes one pip per consumed controller `step_event` at the latched `stride_target_state` radius; **(b) Burst** — a limited-resource throwable point-source. The emitter publishes one **raw `NoisePublished`** payload; Perception (R5) is the sole hearing consumer and creates a separate per-guard **`NoiseHeardRelay`** payload. The emitter **never decides** — it publishes attributable facts (`kind`, `position`, `radius`, `source_timestamp`, `t_publish`, `publisher = NoiseEmitter`, emitter-allocated `fact_id`). Any generic `timestamp` carried on a raw fact or relay is transport/trace metadata only (it may equal `source_timestamp` as a trace alias); it has no gameplay eligibility or ordering authority. Normative hearing eligibility uses `t_publish`; normative fact order is `(source_timestamp, source_event_class_rank, source_event_id, fact_id)` and normative pair order is `(source_timestamp, source_event_class_rank, source_event_id, fact_id, guard_eid)`. Guard reaction (Investigate vs ignore) is FSM-owned. **Two ids, two owners (B2):** the emitter allocates the transport/dedup token **`fact_id`** — one per published fact, shared by every hearing guard (a three-guard broadcast carries one `fact_id`); the **`entry_id`** — the *episode* id — remains **Perception-owned**, allocated at first-noise-heard per guard and relayed downstream by Perception/FSM. The raw emitter payload carries neither `entry_id` nor `consumption`; Perception adds both to each relay. The emitter never allocates episode ids or guard decisions; Perception never allocates a `fact_id`.

2. **Movement emission is a relay of the controller's `step_event` stream — the emitter owns no ledger.** Controller F5 owns the global audible-distance ledger entirely: `L_audible(t+dt) = L_audible(t) + |Δposition_XZ|` in `{Walk, Run}`, frozen (never reset) in `{Idle, Crouch}`, with stride thresholds (`stride_length_walk 1.9 m` / `stride_length_run 2.6 m`), remainder retention, and `audible_armed` (cleared only on capture/respawn). When the ledger commits, the controller publishes **`step_event {session_id, attempt_epoch, step_id, state, previous_position, position, stride_length_m, ledger_remainder_m, timestamp, publisher}`** (C7), at most once per Tick. `step_id` is a monotonic controller-owned source-event identity, unique within `(session_id, attempt_epoch)`; `previous_position` is the post-resolution position from the prior controller tick. Controller F5 is also the sole producer of the monotonic `stride_id` used by audio-only rustle records; `stride_id` is unique within `(session_id, attempt_epoch)` and is not derived from listener arrival or render frames. The emitter deduplicates by `(session_id, attempt_epoch, step_id)` before allocating `fact_id`, so a retry cannot create a second gameplay fact. **For each first-consumed `step_event`, the emitter publishes exactly one raw `NoisePublished` fact:**
   - `stride_target_state = Walk` → `kind = movement`, `movement_mode = Walk`, `resolved_nominal_radius = R_walk 4.0`, `radius = resolved_nominal_radius`, `position = step_event.position` (player feet, world XZ+Y); the observational `state` is retained but does not select the radius
   - `stride_target_state = Run` → `kind = movement`, `movement_mode = Run`, `resolved_nominal_radius = R_run 6.0`, `radius = resolved_nominal_radius`, `position = step_event.position`; the observational `state` is retained but does not select the radius
   - `fact_id` allocated at accepted raw-fact publication (one per fact, shared by all hearing guards), `source_event_class_rank = 0` (movement; Burst = 1), `source_timestamp = step_event.timestamp`, `terminal_publication_time = not applicable`, `fact_publication_state = published`, `publisher = NoiseEmitter`

   The emitter **never** reads or writes `L_audible`, never applies a threshold, never retires a stride, and never runs a timer. It does not read the controller's polled `r_noise` readout (F5g: debug/telemetry only, **never a gameplay channel**). States outside `{Walk, Run}` produce no `step_event` (F5 freezes the ledger; displacement honesty bills actual meters), so the emitter has nothing to relay and publishes nothing — it never synthesizes an event from state alone. A missing, non-finite, non-positive-XZ-displacement, out-of-order, or duplicate source event is rejected/logged as a controller/bus contract failure; it is never converted into a new fact.
   - **Fresh-session commit** is controller-owned (F5c): first `step_event` ≈ **0.551 s** Walk / **0.456 s** Run at starters (movement-noise radii `R_walk 4.0 m` / `R_run 6.0 m`, stride lengths `1.9 m` / `2.6 m`, speeds `3.6` / `6.25 m·s⁻¹). These are the pacing windows the player feels; this GDD consumes them, never re-derives them.

   **Cross-state stride latch.** To prevent Walk↔Run switching from either postponing a near-threshold pip or forcing one immediately, Controller F5 latches `stride_target_state` and `stride_length_m` when a new audible stride first receives positive Walk/Run displacement. The target remains fixed until that stride commits; a state change affects only the observational `state` field and the next stride target. The eventual event's radius and audio blend remain bound to the latched target. Silent-state pauses preserve both progress and the latched target. This is the single threshold policy used by the controller, emitter, audio, and preview.

3. **Burst is a limited-resource, re-arm-at-pickup throwable.** Ownership is split: each level pickup owns its instance state (`Placed` or `Consumed`), while the player owns one `Carried` slot and one active flight handle; Save/Session snapshots the slot and pickup instance ids. MVP Burst lifecycle: **Placed pickup** in the level (exactly two pickups in the one-room MVP profile: one initial gate pickup and one reachable reserve; `pickup-reached` telemetry records radius entry; pickup reach = `pickup_reach_radius 1.6 m [1.0, 2.5]`) → **Carried** (exactly one at a time; the `Throw` input is available) → **In-flight** (parabolic arc, gravity `−9.81 m/s²`, `BurstThrowConfig` starter `v0 = 10.0 m/s @ 38°`, **raised-origin range** `R = v₀·cosθ·(v₀·sinθ + √((v₀·sinθ)² + 2·g·h_release))/g` with `h_release 1.5 m` — starter lands **≈ 11.5 m**, in the `[8, 12]` safe band; collision with `World` stops flight) → **Landed** → emit one raw `NoisePublished` (`kind = burst`, `position = landing point`, `radius = R_burst 10.5 m`) exactly once → **Consumed**. **Throw verb (B7):** the throw launches **instantly on the `Throw` input edge** (no wind-up in MVP), in the **camera azimuth** sampled at the throw edge. **Throw requires a stationary stance (2026-09-01 re-review, game-designer F7 — sprint-and-throw dominant strategy):** the `Throw` edge is accepted only from an authoritative `Idle`/standing stance at resolved planar speed zero, or from `Crouch-Idle` after a successful same-tick stand resolution. Every `Walk`/`Run`-labelled edge is rejected before allocation/spend even when its resolved planar speed is zero; any positive resolved planar speed is rejected regardless of label. Rejected edges produce no spend, flight, audio, or raw fact, and the rejection is logged (same pre-spend rejection family as the blocked-launch leg). This closes the sprint-and-throw combination — running speed plus the full `R_burst` radius in a single action — which removed the intended trade of Run (speed for sound). The launch anchor is `feet + throw_release_height` and is stance-independent for trajectory math, but a crouched player may throw only after a successful same-tick stand resolution; if standing is blocked, the edge is rejected with no spend. This prevents a high-release Burst from being a consequence-free crouch-under-cover action. Before spend, the launch performs the exact initial-overlap operation `PhysicsScene.OverlapSphere(launch_position, projectile_radius, E20_mask, QueryTriggerInteraction.Ignore)` in the injected PhysicsScene. This is an overlap query, not a swept `SphereCast` terminal. If it reports any E20 solid, the Throw edge is rejected as blocked: `Carried` is preserved, no spend/flight/audio/raw fact occurs, and the rejection is logged; certified launch volumes must keep the sphere clear. Otherwise the launch uses the player's resolved post-movement position and velocity snapshot from that tick; the preview samples the same inputs and applies the same preflight rejection. A **persistent** arc + predicted-contact ghost is shown the entire time the Burst is `Carried` (a diegetic aiming affordance, not HUD — revised 2026-08-31: the prior transient throw-moment ghost was useless as an aim aid). Both use the same fixed-step collision preview as the simulator and show the predicted collision-resolved contact or the documented 3-second fallback point; when the throw launches, the ghost resolves into the live flight marker. **Pickup input:** entering reach only exposes the prompt and emits `pickup-reached` telemetry; an `Interact` edge while the pickup is `Placed` transitions it to `Carried`. **Re-arm rule (limited resource):** a Burst is spent at the throw edge and is never restored by death or segment reset. A carried, unthrown Burst survives death via checkpoint snapshot. A landed Burst becomes `Consumed`; an in-flight Burst killed by death also becomes `Consumed` without publishing a noise fact. Segment reset restores only still-unconsumed `Placed` pickups; it never restores a spent pickup instance. The MVP reserve invariant is: after a single failed spend **of the gate pickup** (wall impact, ceiling contact, void fallback, or death during flight), the reserve pickup remains physically reachable from the checkpoint/start route. (If the *reserve* pickup itself is spent, no reserve remains — a player routing failure, not a certification violation; see the MVP Level Authoring Contract below.) There is no cooldown, no ammo pool, no pickup stockpile — pickup geometry is the resource gate. A Burst in flight that lands out of NavMesh or against a wall still emits at the contact point; the hearing Linecast from guards still gates audibility.

   **Immutable `ThrowSnapshot` (shared runtime/preview/fixture input):** at the accepted Throw edge, the authoritative Player/Burst integration is the explicit sole `ThrowSnapshot` producer; it captures exactly one immutable snapshot and passes that same record to runtime simulation, landing-ghost preview, and `LevelFixture`. The schema is `{session_id, attempt_epoch, flight_handle_id, accepted_edge_timestamp, launch_feet_position, launch_position, resolved_velocity, resolved_stance, camera_azimuth_deg, resolved_facing_azimuth_deg, trajectory_v0_mps, trajectory_theta_deg, throw_release_height_m, projectile_radius_m, source_pickup_id, geometry_variant_id}`. `resolved_velocity` is the authoritative projectile initial velocity vector: it is derived only from the captured `trajectory_v0_mps`, `trajectory_theta_deg`, and camera/facing azimuth, and is consumed identically by runtime, preview, and LevelFixture. Player movement velocity is not added to this vector; if captured for diagnostics it must use a separate non-authoritative field. `launch_position = launch_feet_position + up × throw_release_height`; `camera_azimuth_deg` is normalized to `[0°,360°)` at the input edge and falls back to resolved facing only when planar aim is near zero. All fields are finite, immutable, and captured after same-tick movement/stand resolution; consumers never resample render-frame camera, player velocity, stance, or pickup state. A rejected Throw has no snapshot and no `flight_handle_id`; a death-cancelled flight retains its snapshot and handle but publishes no raw fact. Runtime, preview, and fixture must compare the snapshot field-for-field before accepting parity evidence.

**Movement/Burst boundary.** A Burst throw and landing never enter the movement ledger or the controller's `step_event` path. The Throw edge is sampled in the same authoritative input phase as movement resolution, but it never advances `L_audible`, changes the current `stride_target_state`, changes `stride_length_m`, or creates a movement pip. The Burst source is represented only by its `flight_handle_id` and its own `NoisePublished` at terminal landing; a simultaneous movement commit remains a separate source event and receives its deterministic `source_event_class_rank`.

4. **Two payloads, broadcast, per-guard single consumption, `fact_id` transport.** Every emission publishes one raw `NoisePublished { session_id, attempt_epoch, kind: movement|burst, source: player|burst-landing, position: feet|landing, radius: R_walk|R_run|R_burst, fact_id, fact_publication_state, source_timestamp, terminal_publication_time, t_publish, timestamp, publisher, source_event_class_rank, source_event_id }`. The `kind` enum is canonical lowercase `{movement, burst}` in every raw, relay, gate, and registry contract; display prose may capitalize “Burst” only as the item/system name. The raw `fact_publication_state` is `published|rejected|invalidated`; a published fact has a nonzero emitter-owned `fact_id`. Movement payloads additionally require `movement_mode = Walk|Run` and `resolved_nominal_radius = R_walk|R_run`, with `radius = resolved_nominal_radius`; Perception applies vertical falloff to that resolved payload radius and never infers the mode from generic `kind=movement`. For Movement, the serialized `source_event_id = step:<step_id>` while the bare `step_id` remains the internal dedup/allocation field, `source_timestamp = step_event.timestamp`, `terminal_publication_time = not applicable`, and `t_publish = source_timestamp`; for Burst, the serialized `source_event_id = flight:<flight_handle_id>` while the bare `flight_handle_id` remains the internal allocation field, `source_timestamp` is the accepted throw/input-edge ordering time, `terminal_publication_time` is the landing/timeout publication time, and `t_publish = terminal_publication_time`. Perception creates one `NoiseHeardRelay` per in-range guard by adding `guard_eid`, the Perception-owned per-guard `entry_id`, and `consumption`. **`fact_id` is allocated by the emitter when an accepted raw-fact publication is ingressed** — one id per published fact, and that same id reaches every guard that hears it (a single Burst heard by three guards carries one `fact_id`). **`entry_id` is allocated by Perception at first-noise-heard (R5) and relayed** — the emitter never allocates it. Perception R5 **broadcasts** to **every** in-range guard — one guard's consumption never invalidates another's relay; each guard's FSM consumes its relay at most once per episode; stale relays after `Chase-end`, death, or segment reset are dropped, never re-heard. The Event bus must deliver each raw fact once to each active Perception listener; an at-least-once transport may retry internally only if it deduplicates by `(session_id, attempt_epoch, fact_id)` before fan-out — the epoch component removes any reset-ambiguity so a pooled/reset emitter can never re-deliver a stale fact (resolves the cross-epoch `fact_id` collision risk). Duplicate `step_event` publications are rejected by `(session_id, attempt_epoch, step_id)` before `fact_id` allocation. `attempt_epoch` increments **exactly once per atomic lifecycle transition** — one increment covers the death/capture terminal transition *including* the respawn that follows it, and one covers a segment reset (the Pass A lifecycle table below is the authoritative count; the prior prose list read as if death, capture, respawn, and reset were four increments); Perception discards queued facts from older epochs at the transition boundary. *(Cross-doc amendment completed: perception.md and `entities.yaml` distinguish the raw publication from the per-guard relay; `entry_id` remains Perception-owned and the relay additionally retains emitter-owned `fact_id`.)*

5. **Hearing geometry is Perception-owned; the emitter declares the source-specific origin.** The emitter publishes the noise position in world space (movement: `step_event.position` at the player's feet; Burst: the pushed-out landing contact). Perception F12 performs the hearing evaluation: for `movement`, ray origin is **feet + `f12_noise_origin_offset` 0.25 m** (clears a 0.20 m stair riser); for `Burst`, ray origin is the published landing contact itself (the Burst position is already the post-hit pushed-out sphere center, so no player-feet offset is added). Ray endpoint is **guard eye** (no ear bone — eye-anchor is the canonical perception operand), with **binary Linecast, no partial attenuation** (B9 — a portal/gap either passes sound at full radius or blocks at zero; there is no in-between), cast on the **shared E20 mask**. E20 is a serialized bitmask resolved once from the exact project layer names defined by Physics; it includes every `World` layer and excludes `Player`, `Guard`, and `trigger` layers. The resolver validates each required name via `LayerMask.NameToLayer(name) ≥ 0` (fail load if any returns −1 — the layer is missing from the project layer list), asserts the assembled mask != 0, and asserts no forbidden-layer bit (`Player`, `Guard`, `trigger`) is set; exact layer names are resolved from the Physics project layer list, not a `World` family placeholder, and the cached mask is never rebuilt per query. The mask is also asserted **content-ful at scene load**: the loaded scene must contain at least one non-trigger collider on the mask, or E20 queries are meaningless (a serialized bitmask that matches no geometry silently passes every occlusion check) — validated once at scene load, not per query. The Physics ADR additionally pins `Physics.queriesHitBackfaces = false` and the mesh-winding policy for E20 solids: a backface hit would leak hearing through single-sided authored geometry (see OQ2). The same resolved mask serves vision rays, hearing, and the Burst projectile. `QueryTriggerInteraction.Ignore` is passed on every query. **NavMesh-on-World authoring rule (E20a):** NavMesh colliders must NOT share a layer with E20 World-layer physical solids. The **Burst** initial-overlap preflight uses the exact `PhysicsScene.OverlapSphere` operation on the resolved World mask with `QueryTriggerInteraction.Ignore`; only the subsequent projectile sweep uses a closest-hit `Physics.SphereCast`; the **hearing** occlusion query is the binary `Physics.Linecast` (any-hit semantics — a phantom blocker anywhere along the segment blocks hearing, which is why the rule protects hearing too, not only the projectile). If a NavMesh collider is on the same layer as a physical floor, the Burst closest-hit returns the NavMesh slab edge at a grazing angle and the published contact point is laterally shifted by `projectile_radius + epsilon_contact`, while the hearing Linecast falsely blocks through the walkable surface. The `LevelFixture` validation leg `navmesh_layer_separation_check` enumerates every collider in the level scene and fails closed if any NavMesh collider's layer intersects the E20 World mask; the recovery path is to assign the NavMesh colliders to a dedicated `NavMesh` layer that is excluded from the E20 mask. The same rule applies to held props, IK-attached bones, and any other collider that could sit on the `World` layer in standard AI Navigation authoring (authoring-only helper colliders are never gameplay occluders). **Hearing distance is planar (B10):** `d_noise` is the **XZ-plane** distance from guard to noise origin, with a **soft Y-delta falloff** that hits 0% at `|ΔY| = hearing_y_hard_cutoff 4.0 m [3, 5]` (renamed from `hearing_y_tolerance` to reflect the soft-leg semantics), where `ΔY = |noise_origin_y − guard_feet_y|` — the **hearing datum is the listening guard's feet**, not the eye (re-anchored 2026-08-31 re-review: the prior eye-anchored form multiplied every flat-ground movement pip by `1 − guard_eye_height/hearing_y_hard_cutoff ≈ 0.60`, silently shrinking the nominal 4.0/6.0/10.5 m radii to ≈2.65/3.98/6.43 m effective and breaking the LOCKED walk:Burst radius ratio; the eye remains the **occlusion ray endpoint**, never the falloff operand), with `noise_origin_y = feet_y + f12_noise_origin_offset` for movement and `noise_origin_y = landing_contact_y` for Burst. On flat ground the starter effective radii are `R_walk_eff ≈ 3.75 m`, `R_run_eff ≈ 5.63 m`, `R_burst_eff ≈ 10.37 m` (ΔY = 0.25 / 0.25 / ≈0.05 m) — the nominal 4.0/6.0/10.5 values remain the registered radii and the LOCKED ordering/ratio contract. The 0.25 m origin offset, mask pin, occlusion endpoint convention, and `hearing_y_hard_cutoff` remain single-sourced in `entities.yaml` and the Physics ADR.

6. **Noise never charges the accumulator, only Investigate.** Hearing triggers **Investigate/orientation** (FSM consumes the relay), never `dA/dt` and never `R` charge. Sustained LOS after arrival may then charge `A`/`R`, but noise is not a backdoor to Chase (concept: "noise never feeds the accumulator — only LOS does"). `Burst` vs `movement` attribution is carried for the Suspicion Attribution trace (R12) and for the `R_noise_share` consequence below.

7. **Fruitless noise has a bounded in-world consequence.** A noise-triggered Investigate that resolves with the player unfound (`Investigate-resolution cause: fruitless`) accrues **one** bounded share per noise-led Investigate episode via Perception F6: `episode add = min(R_noise_share 0.15, c_noise 0.20)` (effective 0.15 at starters, clamped `R + add ≤ R_max 1.00`). Corroborating facts do not create duplicate resolution records or duplicate residual additions; the corroborated episode applies its one add only when it ultimately resolves fruitless. Camera trips mirror this; LOS exposure still charges `R` in parallel at `r_res 0.10`. This is the anti-puppeting lever — repeated tool use or fruitless luring tightens `T_entry` visibly (sinking marker) without ever directly grade-penalizing clean tool use (residual is excluded from the grade integral). **Corroboration rule:** for a noise-led live episode, a guard that receives a *second* `Noise heard` (any `fact_id`) within `t_noise_corroborate_window 6.0 s` of the episode anchor's source-kind `t_publish` in the **same investigative area**, where same-area is measured from the first qualifying raw noise origin in XZ (`d_noise_origin ≤ t_noise_corroborate_radius 1.5 m` — a registered twin of `r_investigate_error` with a distinct corroboration semantic), **escalates** to the FSM's **peer-recruit** leg instead of debouncing. For a live non-noise-led episode, the episode-open commit position is the anchor: hide-entry uses the authored hide-spot position, cap-forced uses the cap-reached position; the comparison window starts at that episode-open commit's `t_publish`, and the candidate's raw origin is compared to that fixed XZ anchor. The guard recruits a peer if available and eligible; the original Investigate remains open until peer arrival or window expiry. A *third* same-area fact within the same window escalates to **locked zone**. These Target-tier outcomes are outside the MVP source/response boundary below. These corroboration exceptions take precedence over the generic live-episode drop; unrelated facts and facts outside the window are consumed without effect. The peer-recruit/locked-zone escalation is Target-tier and requires the Alert Propagation/Level zone contract. **MVP single-guard corroboration (no coordination service):** a second/third same-area `Noise heard` during a live noise-led Investigate is NOT blanket-dropped and does NOT escalate to a peer — the single guard **re-anchors** its search to the new noise commit origin, performs a brief re-orient toward that origin, and **re-arms its give-up budget** to the corroboration formula `t_reanchor` (the FSM D1 budget computed at `R_reanchor` plus the fixed extension `t_noise_reanchor_extend 2.0 s [1.0, 4.0]` — deeper, longer corroborated search; see the re-anchor formula in Pass A) before resuming the live episode. This is the cognitive corroboration effect that keeps the machine "listening" on confirmation (Pillar 1) within the MVP scope — the Pass-2 Pillar-1 violation is resolved here, not deferred. ### Pass A — AI behavior-contract amendment (2026-08-29)

This amendment is normative for the Player Noise, Perception, and Guard AI integration. If an older paragraph describes the same boundary less precisely, this contract wins.

#### Runtime response profile

`noise_response_profile` is an immutable session configuration with exactly two values: `MVP` and `Target`. The loader validates the profile before the first playable tick and records it in every LevelFixture and replay header. `MVP` requires `coordination_service = absent` and permits only the single-guard response: first eligible noise opens Investigate; a second or third qualifying same-area noise re-anchors that guard and never publishes `peer-recruit` or `locked-zone`. `Target` requires a successful AlertPropagation capability/readiness handshake before gameplay starts; peer recruitment and locked-zone decisions are legal only after that handshake. A Target configuration without the service, or a service that reports not-ready, is a validation failure. There is no implicit Target-to-MVP fallback and no fixture may infer the profile from observed behavior. MVP fixtures containing peer-recruit or locked-zone records fail validation.

#### Ownership and relay lifecycle

- **NoiseEmitter** owns source validation, source-event deduplication, raw `NoisePublished` publication, and `fact_id` allocation. Movement deduplicates by `(session_id, attempt_epoch, step_id)` and Burst by `(session_id, attempt_epoch, flight_handle_id)` before a `fact_id` is allocated. It owns neither hearing geometry nor an episode, and it never allocates `entry_id` or a guard decision.
- **Perception** owns the active-guard snapshot, hearing geometry, hearing queue and deadline, relay creation, and `entry_id` reservation. After a fact passes hearing admission for a guard, Perception allocates an `entry_id` only if the latest published FSM liveness fact says that guard has no live episode; otherwise it relays the existing live `entry_id`. This is a reservation, not a decision: the immutable relay is published with `consumption = eligible` before the FSM reads it. If FSM precedence, immunity, cooldown, Chase, or a terminal latch suppresses the relay, FSM records `ignored` (or `stale` after an epoch/lifecycle barrier); an unused reservation is closed in the trace and is never reused for a later episode. Perception never mutates the relay after publication and never synchronously queries the FSM.
- **Guard AI FSM** is the only behavioral consumer. It deduplicates by `(session_id, attempt_epoch, guard_eid, fact_id)`, applies the profile and precedence rules, and publishes the immutable consumption outcome plus any decision record. `consumed` means the relay was applied to the episode or re-anchor; `ignored` means it was read but suppressed; `stale` means it was invalidated before behavior could read it. A duplicate relay produces no second behavior or id.
- **Session/lifecycle owner** is the sole owner of `attempt_epoch`. NoiseEmitter, Perception, and individual FSMs never increment it. The owner performs one atomic transition per lifecycle event according to the canonical lifecycle transition table (reproduced below), invalidates the old epoch before current-epoch sensing/decision work, and carries the new envelope into all subsequent facts and decisions.

  | Event                              | attempt_epoch behavior                                       |
  |------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
  | Death → capture (or vice versa)    | One atomic transition; exactly one increment                |
  | Player respawn (post-death)        | Part of the same atomic transition; no additional increment  |
  | Segment reset (room transition)    | One atomic transition; exactly one increment                |
  | Full-room restart                  | New session; globally fresh, never-reused `session_id`, with fresh `attempt_epoch = 1` |
  | Scene reload                       | New session; fresh `attempt_epoch = 1`                       |
  | New playable attempt               | New session; fresh `attempt_epoch = 1`                       |
  | Pause / resume                     | No increment; virtual clock freezes and resumes              |
  | Pool / unpool                      | No increment; scheduler phase preserved                      |

  **Full-room restart semantics.** Full-room restart clears the carried Burst slot AND restores all authored pickups to Placed. Death/segment-reset preserves the carried slot; a Placed instance that was the source of a carried Burst is not restored while the slot is occupied.

#### One total order for a virtual boundary

All systems use one shared session virtual clock. At each boundary, the authoritative order is:

1. Apply the session/epoch barrier; discard or mark stale every older Event Bus envelope, Perception raw/deferred/work item, and FSM relay.
2. Apply death, capture, respawn, and other terminal latches; terminal facts win over later work from the old lifecycle.
3. Resolve movement, pickup, and input state; validate a Throw, simulate the Burst, resolve collision/timeout, and publish authoritative raw facts at their source-kind publication times `t_publish` (Movement `source_timestamp`; Burst `terminal_publication_time`). A fact published after the hearing drain is queued for the next eligible boundary.
4. Evaluate the due hearing boundary using the separate deterministic keys: guard selection `guard_eid` ascending (with `stable_guard_snapshot_position` recorded but non-ordering), fact ordering `(source_timestamp, source_event_class_rank, source_event_id, fact_id)`, and pair dispatch `(source_timestamp, source_event_class_rank, source_event_id, fact_id, guard_eid)`. The boundary reads the latest liveness fact published before this drain; a liveness fact emitted after the drain is visible at the next boundary.
5. Publish immutable Perception relays and their entry reservations/outcomes; no relay is synchronously pulled by the FSM.
6. Process the FSM's committed decisions and publish every resulting `LivenessFact` (`open`, `promote`, or `close`) before the next hearing drain. The FSM is the sole liveness writer; Perception never synchronously queries it.
7. Resolve FSM-owned timers after facts; a catch completion wins a simultaneous give-up expiry and qualifying same-boundary corroboration precedes give-up expiry.
8. Flush telemetry, audio/UI notifications, and other bookkeeping.

#### Production hearing path (4 stages)

The canonical pipeline that carries a `NoisePublished` from source to a consumed relay
is the **production hearing path**. The four stages have one owner each, one identity
each, and one terminal failure code each:

| # | Stage | Owner | Identity produced | Terminal failure code |
|---|-------|-------|-------------------|----------------------|
| 1 | Source validation + same-timestamp allocation | NoiseEmitter | `fact_id` (emitter-allocated, deterministic) | `burst-launch-blocked` (Burst preflight); no fact for movement rejection |
| 2 | Transactional Event Bus handoff | Event Bus | retained immutable `NoisePublished` envelope (handed off as `handoff_pending` until every active Perception subscription returns a verdict) | `event-bus-downstream-handoff-retry-exhausted` (Bus owns handoff failure) |
| 3 | 5 Hz hearing scheduler admission | Perception (PerceptionHearingService) | `entry_id` (Perception-allocated, only at first-eligible relay of a live episode; otherwise relays the live `entry_id`) | `perception-hearing-deadline-missed` (Perception owns deadline miss) |
| 4 | Relay consumption | Guard AI FSM | `consumed` / `ignored` / `stale` outcome (FSM-owned, separate keyed outcome record; relay is never mutated) | `stale` after epoch/terminal invalidation; `ignored` for live-epoch suppression |

**Identity allocation and propagation order.** `flight_handle_id` is allocated at the accepted Throw edge. `fact_id` is allocated at accepted raw-fact publication. `entry_id` is opaque and Perception-owned: Perception allocates it at hearing admission only when the latest event-sourced FSM liveness snapshot has no live episode. Movement and Burst source identities are disjoint namespaces (`step:<step_id>` and `flight:<flight_handle_id>`), and monotonicity is per namespace within `(session_id, attempt_epoch)`. Identities are then propagated in this exact order: (1) `fact_id` at raw-fact publication, (2) envelope identity `(session_id, attempt_epoch, fact_id)` at Bus ingress, (3) `entry_id` at hearing admission, (4) FSM consumption outcome as a separate keyed record. No identity is re-derived after it is allocated; the FSM never allocates or re-derives `fact_id` or `entry_id`. Stage 2's "active Perception subscription" is the ADR-0001 token subscription that has registered for `NoisePublished` handoff; the per-boundary guard/fact selection is a snapshot of that subscription at the due boundary.

**Liveness snapshot source.** Step 4's "no live episode" reading is the FSM-owned liveness fact documented in `guard-ai-fsm.md §Liveness fact publication` and registered as `liveness_fact_lifecycle` in `entities.yaml`. The liveness window is `[opener, closer]` (opener = `investigate-commit` or `Chase-entry`; `promote` = the Investigate→Chase tier transition retaining the same `entry_id`; closer = `investigate-resolution` / `Chase-end` / `Capture`); outside that window, Perception may reserve a new `entry_id`; inside it, Perception relays the live `entry_id`. Investigate→Chase is one `LivenessFact {op=promote}` transition: it retains the current `entry_id` and episode identity, and must not emit an Investigate close followed by a second Chase open for the same episode.

**Liveness position and stale-close handoff (normative).** Every `LivenessFact.position` is finite world-space data with an explicit `position_source`: `opener_decision` for `op=open` (the matching `investigate-commit.position` or `Chase-entry.position`), `episode_position` for `op=promote` (the last authoritative target/decision position, unchanged by promotion), `terminal_decision` for a normal `op=close` (the matching resolution/`Chase-end`/witness-Capture position), and `last_authoritative_episode_position` for `op=close,cause=stale` (the liveness ledger's last stored position immediately before invalidation). It is never copied from a guard transform, relay arrival, or wall-clock callback; an opener or closer without its required position source is rejected.

The lifecycle/session owner alone increments `attempt_epoch` and owns the atomic barrier. Before invalidating prior-epoch queues, it submits exactly one `StaleLivenessClose` handoff per live `(session_id, attempt_epoch, guard_eid, entry_id)` to `GuardAISystem`; the FSM is delegated to publish exactly one `LivenessFact {op=close, cause=stale, position_source=last_authoritative_episode_position, publisher=GuardAISystem}` for each request. The lifecycle owner does not publish a second close. The handoff is retained/deduplicated by Event Bus key `(session_id, attempt_epoch, guard_eid, entry_id, op, source_timestamp)`, where `source_timestamp` is the exact epoch-transition time; deterministic ordering is `(source_timestamp, guard_eid, entry_id, op)`. The epoch transition may commit only after every stale close is retained or a fail-closed barrier record exists; no current-epoch processing begins before that handoff, and a late duplicate callback is a no-op.
The same order applies at exact timestamp equality. A fact accepted before the boundary's drain is eligible in that boundary; a fact accepted after the drain waits for the next boundary. Pause freezes the virtual clock and does not advance any queue, timer, or scheduler phase. Pooling and `OnEnable`/`OnDisable` do not reset the phase or migrate queued work.

#### Corroboration, re-anchor, and target determinism

The corroboration anchor is selected by the live episode cause: for a **noise-led** episode it is the first raw `NoisePublished.position` and source-kind publication time `t_publish` accepted as the episode's commit; for a non-noise-led `hide-entry` episode it is the authored hide-spot position and episode-open commit `t_publish`; for a non-noise-led `cap-forced` episode it is the cap-reached position and episode-open commit `t_publish`. `t_publish` is `source_timestamp` for Movement and `terminal_publication_time` for Burst. Qualifying facts compare their source-kind publication times and raw XZ origins against that fixed episode-open anchor, not relay arrival time, evaluation time, guard position, or a jittered search target. `source_timestamp` remains ordering-only for Burst and is never substituted for its terminal publication time:

```text
Δt_publish = t_publish(candidate) − t_publish(anchor)
0 ≤ Δt_publish ≤ t_noise_corroborate_window
XZ_distance(candidate_origin, anchor_origin) ≤ t_noise_corroborate_radius
candidate.fact_id ≠ anchor.fact_id  when anchor.kind = noise
true                              when anchor.kind ∈ {hide-entry, cap-forced}
```

The fixture records `episode_anchor_kind ∈ {noise, hide-entry, cap-forced}` and an
`episode_anchor_identity`: the retained anchor `fact_id` for `noise`, or the
`entry_id` plus authored hide-spot/cap transition identity for a non-noise episode.
A candidate relay is still deduplicated by its own `(session_id, attempt_epoch,
guard_eid, fact_id)` tuple; a non-noise anchor is not treated as a fabricated raw fact.

The interval is inclusive at both boundaries. Out-of-order, out-of-window, out-of-radius, duplicate, stale, or already-consumed facts do not advance the corroboration count. The selected episode-open anchor remains the count and timing anchor for the episode. A qualifying MVP re-anchor targets the second/third fact's raw origin exactly, performs the brief orient, retains the original `entry_id` and episode commit identity, and resets the start arm. It captures `R_reanchor = relay.residual_at_hearing` and selects the timer budget as:

```text
t_reanchor = t_giveup_base × s_diff × (1 + k_thorough × (R_reanchor / R_max)) + t_noise_reanchor_extend
```

The **full registered re-anchor domain** is `[3.45, 20.0] s`, spanning the
registered residual, difficulty, and extension domains. The **starter tuning slice**
is approximately `[6.0, 8.0] s` around the starter configuration; it is not the full
validator domain. These are timer-budget ranges, not hearing-latency bounds.

`s_diff` is the **registered difficulty scalar** (guard-ai-fsm.md D1; registry `s_diff 1.0 [0.7, 1.6]`) — the same scalar the uncorroborated D1 budget uses, so `t_reanchor` is exactly the D1-form budget computed at `R_reanchor` **plus the fixed extension**. The timer starts only after the re-anchor target is reached (or the canonical path-end arm is reached), never at relay arrival or commit. The full registered budget domain is `[3.45, 20.0] s`; approximately `[6.0, 8.0] s` is only the starter tuning slice, not an authoring or validator limit. **Revision rationale (2026-08-31 re-review):** the prior `S_DIFF` speed-ratio formulation is struck — its `max()` leg was dead at every reachable configuration (the normal leg already exceeded the floor whenever `s_diff × R ≥ 1`, impossible since `R ≤ R_max = 1.0` and the starter `s_diff = 1.0`), its `r_actual = 0` edge produced a discontinuous cliff (6.0 s fallback vs ≈2.04 s at 5 cm/s), and the speed-ratio is not measurable without sampling-instant ambiguity (approach speed fluctuates around path corners, so the ratio depends on when it is sampled). **Documented sharp edge (exact origin kept, per 2026-08-31 adjudication):** because the budget recomputes at `R_reanchor`, a corroboration whose residual snapshot is *smaller* than the original commit's produces a numerically smaller budget than the episode's original arming; this is intentional — residual-scaled thoroughness follows the latest evidence, and the clock restart at target arrival plus the fixed extension keep corroborated searches effectively longer in practice. AC15b-MVP binds the formula, not a monotonic-budget property. A re-anchor never allocates a new `entry_id` and never publishes a second `investigate-commit` for the same Investigate tier.

The FSM owns Investigate target generation. For an initial noise, alert, or cap-forced target it draws an injected deterministic uniform-area offset:

```text
u1, u2 = next two values from the episode's deterministic stream in [0, 1)
radius  = r_investigate_error × sqrt(u1)
angle   = 2π × u2
offset  = (radius × cos(angle), 0, radius × sin(angle))
target  = raw_origin + offset
```

The production stream is seeded from `(session_id, attempt_epoch, guard_eid, entry_id, fact_id)` and records seed, sample index, and resulting target for replay. Fixtures inject exact offsets or a seed. The fixed fallback order is: sample the generated candidate with `navmesh_sample_maxdistance`; if that fails, sample the raw origin with the same limit; if that fails, navigate to the closest reachable stand-off and arm the canonical path-end termination. No fallback rerolls the offset. MVP re-anchor targets use the raw origin exactly and do not sample or reroll — confirmation localizes precisely because the player just confirmed the area; this asymmetry (initial commit scatters with `r_investigate_error`, corroboration does not) is the documented sharp edge of the 2026-08-31 adjudication, not an oversight. Perception carries measurements only; it never chooses a target or RNG stream.

#### Hearing endpoint datum

Two distinct vertical datums exist and must never be conflated (2026-08-31 re-anchor decision):

- **Occlusion ray endpoint (geometric):** `guard_eye_world_y = guard_feet_world_y + guard_eye_height` — `guard_eye_height` is a local offset above the listening guard's feet, not a world-space Y coordinate. The binary hearing Linecast runs from the raised noise origin to this eye point.
- **R_eff falloff operand (audibility):** `ΔY = |noise_origin_y − guard_feet_y|` — the **guard's feet**, not the eye. Anchoring the falloff at the eye would multiply every flat-ground movement pip by `1 − guard_eye_height / hearing_y_hard_cutoff ≈ 0.60` and silently shrink the registered radii; the feet anchor keeps the registered radii as the audibility datum (see CR5 and E16 for the effective-radii table).

Perception derives both at the hearing evaluation boundary from the registered guard-foot transform. Movement uses `noise_origin_y = player_feet_world_y + f12_noise_origin_offset`; Burst uses the published landing-contact Y. The resulting `ΔY` is the only vertical operand in `R_eff`; no component may compare a noise world Y directly with the registry heights. When `|ΔY| ≥ hearing_y_hard_cutoff`, `R_eff = 0` and no hearing evaluation occurs — the `max(0, ...)` form makes this explicit; a noise source at or beyond the vertical cutoff is never heard.

### F2 — Commit-to-heard latency (consumed, not derived)

F2 hearing uses the source-kind publication time, not a single timestamp for every source.

| Source kind | Source identity | `source_timestamp` meaning | `terminal_publication_time` | `t_publish` for hearing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Movement | `step:<step_id>` | committed controller step time (`step_event.timestamp`) | not applicable | `source_timestamp` |
| Burst | `flight:<flight_handle_id>` | accepted throw/input-edge ordering only | terminal resolution time (collision/timeout publication time, or death-cancellation time) | `terminal_publication_time` for a published Collision/Timeout fact; not applicable for DeathCancelled |

Burst hearing uses `burst_hearing_deadline = t_publish + T_hearing = terminal_publication_time + T_hearing` only for a published Collision or Timeout fact, never for a DeathCancelled terminal. A DeathCancelled terminal records its cancellation time for lifecycle/trace ordering, emits no `NoisePublished` fact, and creates no hearing deadline. `source_timestamp` remains the Burst allocation and ordering key only; the input-edge timestamp is never substituted for `terminal_publication_time` when calculating hearing eligibility or corroboration.

**Named expression — F2 hearing-boundary timing**

```text
t_publish  = source_timestamp                 for Movement
             = terminal_publication_time      for a published Burst Collision/Timeout fact
             = not applicable                  for a DeathCancelled Burst terminal
t_audible  = b_n(φ) = φ + n·T_hearing,
             n = min{n ∈ ℤ | b_n(φ) ≥ t_publish}
φ          = session hearing phase, fixed for the session/scene and φ ∈ [0, T_hearing)
0 ≤ t_audible − t_publish ≤ T_hearing
```

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| `t_publish` | float | finite, session virtual time | Source-kind publication time used by hearing; `source_timestamp` for Movement and `terminal_publication_time` for Burst |
| `source_timestamp` | float | finite, session virtual time | Movement commit time or Burst accepted-throw/input-edge ordering time; Burst never uses it as its hearing deadline anchor |
| `terminal_publication_time` | float | finite, session virtual time or not applicable | Burst terminal resolution time: collision/timeout publication time for a published fact, or death-cancellation time for a cancelled terminal; not applicable to Movement |
| `T_hearing` | float | `0.2 s` registered | Maximum gameplay-time interval to the next hearing boundary |
| `φ` | float | `[0, T_hearing)` | Fixed session/scene hearing phase |
| `t_audible` | float | `[t_publish, t_publish + T_hearing]` | Hearing-boundary time at which the eligible fact is evaluated |

**Output range:** `t_audible` is bounded and inclusive: it is no earlier than `t_publish` and no later than `t_publish + T_hearing`. An exact-boundary publication is drained in that boundary's deterministic queue phase. F2b converts queue/work-cap overload into an explicit failure rather than silently admitting a late relay.

**Worked examples:** a Movement commit with `source_timestamp = 1.010 s` and the next boundary at `1.200 s` yields `t_publish = 1.010 s`, `t_audible = 1.200 s`, and `Δt = 0.190 s ≤ 0.200 s`. A Burst accepted at `source_timestamp = 0.400 s` that lands and publishes at `terminal_publication_time = 2.400 s` yields `burst_hearing_deadline = 2.600 s`; the throw timestamp does not start the hearing deadline.

The emitter publishes synchronously with the Movement `step_event` or Burst terminal publication; hearing cannot occur earlier than publication and is witnessed on Perception's dedicated 5 Hz hearing sub-evaluation — **not** the 2–5 Hz vision tick (`T_sample` stays a vision-cadence bound only). The bound is a gameplay-time contract; wall-clock delivery during a render hitch is covered by the performance gate, not this formula. This GDD does not recompute Movement `t_commit`; Controller F5c is the single source (the noise GDD's prior `0.528/0.416 s` values contradicted the Approved controller and are struck).

### F2b — Bounded hearing admission and work (consumed, not owned)

The latency bound has a deterministic success-or-explicit-failure contract under the
registered caps. The Event Bus retains an admitted immutable `NoisePublished` envelope
in `handoff_pending` until every active Perception-listener snapshot returns
`Accepted` or `Duplicate`; a full Perception queue returns `Retry`, so the Bus keeps
the same envelope and identity for the registered retry count. Only after all listener
acknowledgements does the Bus mark delivery complete. Retry never allocates a new
`fact_id`, and a terminal handoff failure records
`event-bus-downstream-handoff-retry-exhausted` rather than pretending the fact was
heard.

At a due boundary Perception takes one stable guard snapshot. Guard selection sorts by
`guard_eid` ascending **as its sole key**; `stable_guard_snapshot_position` remains
recorded snapshot data only and is never a sort operand. The snapshot is shared by all
facts in that boundary. Fact admission retains its separate fact ordering key `(source_timestamp, source_event_class_rank, source_event_id, fact_id)`. Pair dispatch retains its separate key
`(source_timestamp, source_event_class_rank, source_event_id, fact_id, guard_eid)`. These are three distinct ordering domains:
guard selection, fact ordering, and pair dispatch. Listener/thread arrival order,
subscription churn, and queue insertion order are never identity sources. The source
fields remain explicit before the guard tie-break; no key is reduced to a suffix-only ordering.

Perception selects at most `max_hearing_guards_per_boundary` guards, evaluates at
most `max_hearing_facts_per_boundary` facts, and executes at most
`max_hearing_pairs_per_boundary` guard/fact pairs. Unselected guards, unevaluated
facts, and unevaluated pairs remain in the bounded ordered
`perception_hearing_work_capacity` queue and are drained before newly arrived work.
A full raw-fact, deferred-boundary, or work queue retries deterministically before
returning its registered rejection diagnostic; no unevaluated pair is silently
removed. Retry exhaustion is evaluated against the same terminal/epoch barrier as
the hearing deadline: epoch invalidation and Burst terminal invalidation win before
retry rejection or deadline admission when they resolve at the same virtual time.
A pair from an invalidated epoch, a `DeathCancelled` terminal, or any terminal
publication state other than `published` is stale and produces no relay.

For an admitted guard/fact pair, the source-kind deadline is `t_publish + T_hearing`,
where `t_publish` is `source_timestamp` for Movement and
`terminal_publication_time` for Burst. The pair either gets its normal relay
evaluation at the next eligible boundary (or while draining ordered deferred work
before that deadline), or is marked `perception-hearing-deadline-missed` with its
identity, boundary, retry count, and queue state and produces no relay. Thus
backpressure cannot create an unbounded late-heard result: overload is an explicit,
measurable failure rather than a hidden relaxation of `T_hearing`.

**Queue rejection record contract.** Every terminal rejection records
`queue_item_kind`; for a guard/fact pair it also records `guard_eid`, alongside
`fact_id`, source identity (`source_event_id` and source kind), `session_id`,
`attempt_epoch`, the source timestamp and derived deadline, retry count, queue name,
queue depth, queue capacity, queue state, and stable `rejection_code`. Reject-newest
preserves older queued work; the rejected item produces no relay, decision, or
passing evidence.

**Worked example:** starter values give `cadence_walk = 3.6 / 1.9 ≈ 1.8947 pips/s`
and `cadence_run = 6.25 / 2.6 ≈ 2.4038 pips/s`, so

**Joint validator legs (shared config validator — every loaded config, asserted before gameplay starts):**
1. **Radius ordering (HARD — load rejection):** `R_walk < R_run < R_burst ≤ R_vis` in the loaded config. Band corners that violate (e.g. `R_burst 14 > R_vis 10`) are **rejected at load**, not silent — the independent safe ranges admit illegal corners by design and the validator is the gate, exactly as `V_crouch < V_patrol` is asserted.
2. **Stride ordering (HARD):** `stride_length_run > stride_length_walk` (the controller/config validator owns the shared check; this GDD co-signs).
3. **Cadence monotonicity (HARD — load rejection, F3(3) adjudication 2026-08-27):** `cadence_ratio = cadence_walk / cadence_run ≈ 0.79` for starter values, and `cadence_ratio ≤ 1` (equivalently `V_walk / stride_length_walk ≤ V_run / stride_length_run`) is asserted at load. Starters `3.6/1.9 = 1.89 ≤ 6.25/2.6 = 2.40` ✓; the inversion corner Fast Walk `4.4/1.5 = 2.93` vs Slow Run `5.0/3.2 = 1.56` is **rejected at load** because a "safer" state emitting pips more often than a "louder" state breaks Pillar 2 (Fair Mind-Challenge) readability — the player cannot predict commit cadence from a slower-and-quicker state. `cadence_walk`, `cadence_run`, and `cadence_ratio` are also **ratio-logged** on the load report, with the comparison result and rejection code, for the Pillar-2 audit trail. The prior ADVISORY downgrade (B3) is **struck**. **Differentiation margin (2026-09-01 re-review, game-designer F2):** a second HARD leg asserts `cadence_ratio ≤ 0.85` — a ratio arbitrarily close to 1.0 passes the monotonicity leg while collapsing the cadence differentiation the player relies on to predict commit timing (at `cadence_ratio = 0.95`, Walk and Run emit pips at nearly identical frequencies). The margin keeps retunes readable; starters at `0.79` pass with room. A config in `(0.85, 1.0]` is rejected at load with its own rejection code on the load report.
4. **Finite-domain validation (HARD):** all radii, speeds, stride lengths, `v0`, `g`, `h_release`, `dt_max`, `projectile_radius`, and `epsilon_contact` must be finite and strictly positive; `R_vis` must be finite and positive; `hearing_y_hard_cutoff` and `guard_eye_height` must be finite and strictly positive (they bound the `R_eff` falloff and the occlusion ray endpoint — a zero or negative value would degenerate the falloff or the ray); `θ` must be finite and in the authored design domain `0° < θ ≤ 45°`. The runtime validator evaluates only global runtime values and the same-height F4 range. Level/content certification separately validates each authored `h_landing`, `Δy_ballistic`, discriminant, actual range, ceiling clearance, and landing envelope; a bad volume is a content-certification failure, not a hidden runtime correction. The 45° upper bound is a deliberate content/readability cap, not a physics requirement. NaN, infinity, zero, negative, out-of-band range, and impossible-height inputs are rejected before gameplay starts with a stable failure code; no value is silently clamped.
5. **Authored throw-volume range gate (HARD — content load rejection):** every `level_burst_route.throw_volume` and `landing_envelope` entry must satisfy its F4-computed `R_actual ∈ [8, 12]` (flat-ground or unequal-height oracle with `D ≥ 0`); an entry whose discriminant `D < 0` or whose computed range falls outside the band is rejected at content load. This extends the flat-only Band certification to non-flat authored volumes so a mezzanine throw cannot produce out-of-band range unflagged at runtime (resolves the E16 atrium-feasibility vs F4-band gap); F3(4)'s `θ` cap is checked independently from this range gate: every loaded `θ` must satisfy `0° < θ ≤ 45°` whether or not its computed range lies in `[8, 12]`. Level/content certification (AC20) asserts both legs independently for every entry. **Band-domain θ gate (2026-09-01 re-review, systems-designer CRITICAL F1):** the open domain `0° < θ ≤ 45°` cannot reach `R_actual ∈ [8, 12]` at shallow angles — at θ→0° the formula collapses to the flat form `R → v₀·√(2·g·h_release)/g`, which tops out at ≈6.18 m at the maximum corner (v0=10.2, h_release=1.8), 23% below the 8 m floor. Every authored throw volume must therefore record its **band-reachable θ domain** — the closed sub-intervals of `0° < θ ≤ 45°` at which that volume's F4-computed `R_actual` stays within `[8, 12]`, computed from the authored `v0`/`h_release`/`h_landing` — and content certification rejects any volume whose authored θ falls outside its recorded intervals (rejection code `AUTHORED_THETA_OUTSIDE_BAND_DOMAIN`, distinct from the F3(4) domain-cap rejection `INVALID_ANGLE`). At the starter volumes (v0=10.0, h_release=1.5): flat ground (h_landing=0) the R=8 crossing is at **θ ≈ 12.66°** and R stays below the 12 m ceiling through the 45° cap (max ≈ 11.60 m at θ ≈ 41.3°), so the band-reachable interval is **[12.66°, 45°]**; the lower-landing volume (h_landing=0.25) crosses R=8 at **θ ≈ 14.75°** with max ≈ 11.38 m, interval **[14.75°, 45°]**. At the top of the registered v0 band (v0=10.2) R exceeds 12 m in a narrow window near θ ≈ 41.4°, so a volume's band-reachable domain may be non-contiguous — the recorded value is a union of closed intervals, never a single assumed interval. The per-volume interval is the authoring operand (it tells the author the usable θ window before any throw is authored); the hard rejection remains this gate plus the F3(5) `R_actual` range gate, and the global `0° < θ ≤ 45°` cap in F3(4) remains the outer content cap.

### F4 — Burst throw range (locked, `BurstThrowConfig`)

**Unequal-height certification (special case, not a runtime correction):** for Y-up launch and landing heights, the Level fixture evaluates `Δy_ballistic = h_release − h_landing`, `D = (v0·sinθ)² + 2·g·Δy_ballistic`, and rejects `D < 0`. It then forms `t_minus = (v0·sinθ − √D)/g` and `t_plus = (v0·sinθ + √D)/g`, discards roots `≤ 0`, and selects the **minimum strictly positive root** as `t_land`; if no such root remains, the volume is rejected. This minimum-positive-root rule is shared by runtime, preview, and Level certification; a later descending crossing is not selected implicitly. Root validity follows two regimes, and selection—not root count—provides uniqueness: when `Δy ≥ 0`, exactly one root is strictly positive (`t_plus`); when `Δy < 0` and `D > 0`, both `t_minus` and `t_plus` are strictly positive and the minimum rule selects the ascending crossing `t_minus`; when `D = 0`, the repeated root is accepted exactly when it is strictly positive; when `D < 0`, the volume is rejected. After discarding non-positive roots, the minimum-strictly-positive selection is unique, including the repeated-root case. A repeated root at `t = 0` or below is rejected by the same `t > 0` predicate, not because repeated roots are categorically invalid. The two roots also satisfy the exact proof identity `t_minus × t_plus = -2 × ΔY_root / g`; this identity is a validator diagnostic, not a substitute for the positive-root predicate. It then computes `R_actual = v0·cosθ·t_land`. Heights are world-space Y values: flat ground in the MVP fixture is `h_landing = 0`, while `h_landing = h_release` denotes equal-height landing at the release plane (`Δy = 0`), not flat ground unless the release plane is world Y=0. Every non-flat throw volume records `h_release`, `h_landing`, `Δy_ballistic`, `D`, the selected root, `R_actual`, ceiling/lateral clearance, and the allowed landing envelope; no unrecorded runtime height adjustment is permitted.

```
R = v₀·cosθ·(v₀·sinθ + √((v₀·sinθ)² + 2·g·h_release)) / g   # raised origin, same height — flat-ground case; unequal-height oracle uses minimum strictly positive root selection
R      ∈ [8, 12] m               # safe band (readability: short enough to demand positioning, long enough to pull across a room)
v0     = 10.0 m/s                # starter (BurstThrowConfig; per-segment tunable)
θ      = 38°                     # starter (BurstThrowConfig)
h_release = 1.5 m                # throw release height above feet — stance-independent (B1)
g      = 9.81 m/s²               # positive gravity magnitude; acceleration vector is (0, -g, 0)
dt_max = 1/120 s                  # arc integration fixed substep (no async, no adaptive step — locked)
projectile_radius = 0.05 m         # single-hit SphereCast projectile radius (pinned, Blocker 3)
epsilon_contact = 0.001 m           # post-hit center push-out margin; Physics ADR/registry co-sign
contact_ambiguity_tolerance = 0.001 m # content-only equal-distance tie tolerance; Physics/Level registry pin
```

|--------|------|-------|-------------|

Starter expected range: `10.0·cos38°·(10.0·sin38° + √((10.0·sin38°)² + 2·9.81·1.5))/9.81 ≈ 11.5 m` — the release height adds ≈1.6 m over the flat-ground form (`v0²·sin(2θ)/g ≈ 9.9 m`, **struck: the emitter throws from chest height, so the flat form underestimates**). Band ⇒ `v0 ∈ [8.1, 10.2] m/s` at the locked `θ = 38°`, `h_release 1.5 m`. The vertical rise above release is `(v0·sinθ)²/(2g) ≈ 1.93 m`; the projectile-center apex is therefore `h_release + 1.93 ≈ 3.43 m` above feet. This absolute apex is the ceiling-clearance datum for E19. **Three-tier tolerance (B1):** (1) **pure-math** — landing range vs formula at 1e-6 relative; (2) **exactly-once heard** at contact (AC6); (3) gameplay contact/position comparisons use the registered `gameplay_contract` `CompareTolerance` (≤5 mm), while `δ_sim = v0·cosθ·dt_max + 1e-3 ≈ 0.067 m` is a diagnostic discrete-step envelope only and is never a placement, route, or gameplay acceptance tolerance; vertical and collision tolerances are covered by the explicit `epsilon_contact`. **Simulator contract:** `dt_max = 1/120 s` is a **fixed substep** (not adaptive or frame-coupled). Per substep, semi-implicit Euler applies `v_next = v_current + gravity·dt`, then `p_next = p_current + v_next·dt`; the segment `p_current → p_next` is swept before committing `p_next`. Render-frame duration never changes the virtual trajectory; required substeps are run in order with no dropped or stretched simulation time while the fixed-step backlog is below `max_backlog_ticks`. If that cap is reached, only accumulated Burst backlog time beyond `max_backlog_ticks × dt_max` is discarded (it never advances `flight_elapsed`) and a `burst-backlog-clamped` diagnostic is recorded; already-published sensing facts remain queued and ordered, and are not discarded by the cap. The preview uses the same equations and collision resolver. Transform synchronization occurs once before each simulation batch (bounded in the Burst budget). The collision query is the single-hit `Physics.SphereCast` (closest-hit) with an initial-overlap check at flight start only — the `SphereCastNonAlloc`/full-buffer nearest-hit form is struck by the F4 collision-authority correction. **Range-band policy:** the certified `R_actual ∈ [8, 12] m` band applies to **every** valid authored throw volume, including unequal-height launch and landing surfaces. The raised-origin closed form above is only the `h_landing = 0` case; a non-flat volume must use the unequal-height oracle with its authored `h_landing`, `Δy_ballistic`, discriminant, and minimum strictly positive root. A 0.20 m stair riser can make a flat-ground estimate exceed `δ_sim`, so the validator rejects a volume unless the actual-height oracle itself is in band; there is no unrecorded runtime height adjustment. Each volume records explicit ceiling/lateral clearance and a landing envelope. Level tunes `v0/θ` per segment so the selected root remains in band; the burst reachable-guard count and the one certified Burst-only route per MVP room are verified against that volume's authoritative formula. Flight is kinematic via the single-hit `Physics.SphereCast` (no Rigidbody), `projectile_radius = 0.05 m`; a preflight initial-overlap hit rejects the Throw before spend, while later collision stops flight and `position = surface_contact + normal × (projectile_radius + epsilon_contact)`.

**B1/B10 surface-vs-atrium clarification (2026-08-30):** the `[8, 12] m` band is a **readability** band, not a physics band. Its purpose is to keep every authored throw short enough to demand positioning (anti-cheese) and long enough to pull across a room (Pillar 3 viability). For a *flat-ground* volume (`h_landing = 0` in the fixture's world-space convention), the raised-origin closed form above gives the unique positive `R_actual`; for an *equal-height* volume (`h_landing = h_release`, hence `Δy = 0`) the same-height ballistic form applies, and for an *atrium/unequal-height* volume (`|Δy| > 0`) the unequal-height oracle yields an `R_actual` that may differ from the flat-ground estimate, and may be slightly shorter (negative `Δy_ballistic`, target on a higher surface) or slightly longer (positive `Δy_ballistic`, target on a lower surface). An atrium volume is certified when (a) its `D ≥ 0`, (b) its **actual-height-oracle** `R_actual` lies in the same `[8, 12] m` readability band, (c) ceiling/lateral clearance and `landing_envelope` are recorded, and (d) the `θ` cap is independently verified — all four legs together. The band is **not** applied as a flat-ground projection to unequal-height volumes; the actual-height oracle is authoritative, and any out-of-band result is rejected by the F3(5) gate rather than hidden by runtime height adjustment. The MVP certification record (`design/levels/mvp-burst-route-fixture.md`, schema `WW-MVP-BURST-ROUTE-1.2`) is flat-ground at the one-room scope, so the closed-form range is the live result; an atrium would appear only at vertical-slice or target tier, where F3(5) governs.

**F4 collision authority correction (2026-08-29):** the earlier `SphereCastNonAlloc` wording and its full-buffer nearest-hit fallback are struck. The normative query is a single-hit `Physics.SphereCast` with Unity closest-hit semantics plus an explicit initial-overlap check:

```text
origin      = p_current
direction   = normalize(p_next − p_current)
maxDistance = |p_next − p_current|
radius      = projectile_radius
mask        = validated E20 World mask
triggers    = QueryTriggerInteraction.Ignore
```

The cast travels exactly the projectile-center substep; `projectile_radius` is the
cast shape radius, not extra travel distance. For this contract Unity's
`RaycastHit.point` is named `collider_surface_contact`: the world-space point on the
hit collider surface, paired with the outward `hit.normal` returned by the closest-hit
SphereCast. The published projectile center is therefore
`collider_surface_contact + hit.normal × (projectile_radius + epsilon_contact)`.
`epsilon_contact` is **only** the post-hit center push-out margin. The separate
`contact_ambiguity_tolerance` is **only** a Level/content-certification tolerance:
two authored solids whose resolved hit distances differ by no more than that value
fail certification rather than choosing an arbitrary collider. A zero-length center
segment skips the sweep after the initial-overlap check. A full-buffer capacity is not
a correctness argument. The projectile center is the trajectory/apex reference,
while sphere-top clearance is `center_apex + projectile_radius`. Runtime, preview,
and LevelFixture use this same resolver; Player Noise never toggles the global
`Physics.autoSyncTransforms` setting per query.

**Named expression — F4 root-product proof**

```text
t_minus × t_plus = -2 × ΔY_root / g
```

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| `t_minus` | float | finite, discarded when `≤ 0` | Lower ballistic root before positive-root filtering |
| `t_plus` | float | finite, discarded when `≤ 0` | Upper ballistic root before positive-root filtering |
| `ΔY_root` | float | finite, authored | `h_release − h_landing` used only by the root-product proof |
| `g` | float | finite, `> 0` | Positive gravity magnitude |
| `t_minus × t_plus` | float | finite when inputs are valid | Root-product diagnostic result |

**Output range:** the root-product result is finite for valid finite inputs and is
negative when `ΔY_root > 0`, zero when `ΔY_root = 0`, and positive when `ΔY_root < 0`. It proves the
root regimes but never overrides the requirement that the selected root be strictly
positive.

**Worked example:** with `ΔY_root = 1.0 m` and `g = 9.81 m/s²`, the identity gives
`t_minus × t_plus = -2 × 1.0 / 9.81 ≈ -0.20387 s²`, so the roots have opposite
signs and only the positive root can be selected.

**F4 validator precedence (first failure wins):** every throw volume is validated in
this exact order: (1) configuration/domain validation; (2) angle validation; (3)
discriminant and strictly-positive-root validation; (4) readable-range validation;
(5) authored-geometry validation. The first failing predicate supplies the stable
rejection code; later predicates do not replace or mask it.

### F5 — Pickup reach admission (shared prompt/`Interact` predicate)

The prompt and the `Interact` edge use one identical 3D sphere-plus-occlusion query. Neither path may substitute horizontal/vertical component tests, a pickup-specific radius, or a different origin.

**Named expression — F5 pickup reach**

```text
pickup_reachable ⇔ distance_squared(player_feet, pickup_anchor) ≤ pickup_reach_radius²
                  AND Linecast_clear(player_feet + up × f12_noise_origin_offset,
                                     pickup_anchor,
                                     E20_mask,
                                     QueryTriggerInteraction.Ignore)
```

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| `player_feet` | `Vector3` | finite world-space position | Player feet position at the authoritative reach evaluation |
| `pickup_anchor` | `Vector3` | finite world-space position | Authored pickup interaction anchor |
| `distance_squared(player_feet, pickup_anchor)` | float | `[0, ∞)` | Full 3D squared distance, including Y |
| `pickup_reach_radius` | float | registered `1.6 m [1.0, 2.5]` | Shared sphere radius |
| `f12_noise_origin_offset` | float | registered `0.25 m` | Vertical lift applied to the Linecast origin |
| `E20_mask` | bitmask | validated nonzero mask | Exact project World layers, excluding Player, Guard, and trigger layers |
| `Linecast_clear(...)` | bool | `{true, false}` | E20 Linecast result with `QueryTriggerInteraction.Ignore` |
| `pickup_reachable` | bool | `{true, false}` | Whether both the sphere and clear-Linecast legs pass |

**Output range:** `pickup_reachable` is boolean. It is true only when the inclusive 3D squared-distance boundary and the clear E20 Linecast both pass; otherwise it is false. The prompt and `Interact` therefore cannot disagree because they consume the same predicate and captured geometry.

**Worked example:** with `pickup_reach_radius = 1.6 m`, `player_feet = (0, 0, 0)`, and `pickup_anchor = (1.2, 0.6, 0)`, `distance_squared = 1.2² + 0.6² = 1.80 m² ≤ 2.56 m²`, so the sphere leg passes. If the E20 Linecast is clear, `pickup_reachable = true`; if a solid blocks the Linecast, `pickup_reachable = false` despite the sphere leg passing.

### F6 — Fruitless residual application (Perception-owned)

NoiseEmitter does not own residual state. When a noise-led Investigate resolves with
`cause=fruitless`, Perception records the nominal eligible share once and applies the
clamped residual delta once for the episode.

**Named expression — F6 nominal share and applied residual delta**

```text
q              = min(R_noise_share, c_noise)
applied_delta  = R_after − R_before = min(q, max(0, R_max − R_before))
R_after        = R_before + applied_delta
```

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| `R_noise_share` | float | registered `[0.10, 0.25]` | Nominal noise share eligible for one fruitless episode |
| `c_noise` | float | registered `[0.15, 0.30]` | Per-episode cap |
| `q` | float | `≥ 0`, at most `min(R_noise_share, c_noise)` | Nominal eligible share recorded once before the residual ceiling clamp |
| `R_before` | float | `[0, R_max]` | Residual immediately before the fruitless resolution |
| `R_max` | float | finite, `> 0` | Residual ceiling |
| `applied_delta` | float | `[0, q]` | Actual residual change, defined as `R_after − R_before` |
| `R_after` | float | `[0, R_max]` | Residual after the single episode application |

**Output range:** `q` is recorded exactly once per fruitless episode. `applied_delta`
is bounded by both `q` and the remaining capacity below `R_max`; it may be zero when
`R_before = R_max` even though `q` is recorded. No duplicate relay,
corroboration, or resolution record creates a second application.

**Worked example:** with `R_noise_share = 0.15`, `c_noise = 0.20`, `R_max = 1.00`,
and `R_before = 0.95`, `q = min(0.15, 0.20) = 0.15`, while
`applied_delta = min(0.15, 0.05) = 0.05` and `R_after = 1.00`. If
`R_before = 1.00`, `q` remains `0.15` and `applied_delta = 0`.

### F12 — Hearing geometry (Perception-owned)

Perception applies vertical falloff to the movement payload's
`resolved_nominal_radius`; it never infers movement mode from generic
`kind=movement`. Movement payloads must carry `movement_mode` and
`resolved_nominal_radius` explicitly. The guard eye is the occlusion endpoint, while
the guard feet are the vertical-falloff datum.

**Named expression — F12 effective hearing radius and admission**

```text
R_eff = resolved_nominal_radius × max(0, 1 − ΔY / hearing_y_hard_cutoff)
heard ⟺ R_eff > 0 AND d_noise ≤ R_eff AND Linecast_clear(noise_origin, guard_eye)
```

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| `resolved_nominal_radius` | float | registered `R_walk`, `R_run`, or `R_burst`, finite and `> 0` | Source payload radius selected before vertical falloff |
| `movement_mode` | enum | `{Walk, Run}` for movement; not applicable for Burst | Explicit source mode carried by movement payloads |
| `ΔY` | float | `[0, ∞)` | `|noise_origin_y − guard_feet_y|`, using guard feet rather than guard eye |
| `hearing_y_hard_cutoff` | float | registered `4.0 m [3, 5]` | Falloff endpoint where effective radius reaches zero |
| `d_noise` | float | `[0, ∞)` | XZ-plane distance from guard feet to the noise origin |
| `Linecast_clear` | bool | `{true, false}` | Binary E20 `Physics.Linecast` from source-specific origin to guard eye |
| `R_eff` | float | `[0, resolved_nominal_radius]` | Effective radius after soft Y falloff |
| `heard` | bool | `{true, false}` | Hearing admission result |

**Output range:** `R_eff` is clamped to `[0, resolved_nominal_radius]`; it reaches
zero at or beyond `|ΔY| = hearing_y_hard_cutoff`. `heard` is true only when the
positive effective radius, inclusive planar boundary (`d_noise ≤ R_eff`), and binary
Linecast all pass. A blocked Linecast produces false regardless of radius.

**Worked example:** a Walk payload with `movement_mode=Walk`,
`resolved_nominal_radius = R_walk = 4.0 m`, `ΔY = 0.25 m`, and
`hearing_y_hard_cutoff = 4.0 m` has `R_eff = 4.0 × (1 − 0.25/4.0) = 3.75 m`.
At `d_noise = 3.75 m`, it passes the inclusive distance check if the Linecast is
clear; at `d_noise = 3.76 m`, it does not. A Burst payload uses its landing contact
as `noise_origin` and `terminal_publication_time` for timing, not a movement mode.

### Pass A revision B — deterministic admission, terminal timing, and bounded failure (2026-08-29)

This subsection is normative where an earlier Pass A paragraph leaves a same-boundary, terminal-event, queue, or presentation edge implicit.

**Deterministic raw-fact allocation.** The authoritative gameplay/input phase serializes accepted source events before allocating `fact_id`. For equal `source_timestamp`, the allocation key is `(source_timestamp, source_event_class_rank, source_event_id)`, where `movement=0` and `Burst=1`; the internal allocation key uses the stable bare numeric `step_id` or `flight_handle_id`, while serialized `source_event_id` uses `step:<step_id>` or `flight:<flight_handle_id>`. The lowest source-allocation key allocates first; subsequent transport and drain use the complete normative fact order `(source_timestamp, source_event_class_rank, source_event_id, fact_id)`, not a suffix-only key. Equal complete keys are the same source event and are deduplicated, never allocated twice. No render-thread or listener arrival order may allocate a competing id.

**Burst two-timestamp contract (2026-08-31 re-review — replaces the single-timestamp definition).** A Burst terminal record carries **two** virtual-time fields with distinct roles; only a published Collision/Timeout terminal becomes a Burst fact:

- **`source_timestamp` = the input-edge capture time** — the moment the `Throw` button edge was sampled. It is the **allocation and ordering key only**: deterministic same-timestamp allocation order `(source_timestamp, source_event_class_rank, source_event_id)`, dedup identity, and replay comparison. It is **never** the deadline anchor for a Burst — every MVP flight lasts ~0.25–1.5 s, so anchoring the `T_hearing` deadline at the input edge would reject every Burst fact at admission.
- **`terminal_publication_time` = the virtual gameplay time of the flight's terminal resolution** — `contact_event_time` on collision, the timeout boundary on timeout, or the death-cancellation time on `DeathCancelled`. For a published Collision/Timeout terminal, this is assigned to `t_publish` and is the **deadline anchor**: F2b's `T_hearing` deadline computes as `deadline = t_publish + T_hearing` (`= terminal_publication_time + T_hearing` for Burst). A `DeathCancelled` terminal has no `t_publish`, no hearing deadline, and no `NoisePublished` fact. Movement facts keep `source_timestamp` as their `t_publish` value because the commit time *is* the publish time.

The input edge is captured in the same authoritative input phase as the step event. A throw-while-moving captures the thrower's origin position at this same input-edge instant, before any subsequent movement-step or landing simulation. The flight then carries `flight_handle_id` (the handle returned by the `InFlight` transition) and the `fact_id` allocated in deterministic same-timestamp order. `contact_event_time` (when present) is a derived fixed-step event time and is never used as `source_timestamp` for ordering — it feeds `terminal_publication_time`.

**Movement-scoped committed-order gate.** The emitter's flush-side order gate (which rejects a source event that commits out of order against an already-allocated later source) applies **only to the movement stream**, whose `step_event`s commit one per tick in monotonic `step_id` order. A Burst fact is **never** rejected for source order: its flight legitimately spans multiple movement commits, and its ordering is already guaranteed at allocation by the deterministic key `(source_timestamp, source_event_class_rank, source_event_id)` — a movement pip committing mid-flight allocates its own `fact_id` without disturbing the flight's reserved identity. Rejecting a landing Burst because movement pips committed during its flight is a contract violation, not a validation success.

**Dedup namespace disjointness.** Movement serialized identities live in the `step:` namespace (`step:<step_id>`), and Burst serialized identities live in the `flight:` namespace (`flight:<flight_handle_id>`). Internal dedup keys retain the bare numeric IDs but are prefixed by their source namespace; each is unique within `(session_id, attempt_epoch)` and the two serialized namespaces are **disjoint by construction**. A unified keyset that mixes both classes (or a per-boundary handle reset that reuses `flight_handle_id` values across boundaries without the epoch scoping) creates silent cross-class collisions; any implementation that cannot separate the namespaces is a validator failure, and a duplicate rejection across classes must name its namespace in the diagnostic.

**Thrower-velocity / launch-velocity composition.** The Burst projectile's initial velocity vector is computed solely from `BurstThrowConfig` (v0 magnitude) and the captured camera azimuth (XZ direction). The thrower's own run/crouch velocity does **not** add to the projectile's initial velocity. The thrower's velocity affects only the *origin position* of the launch (sampled at the input-edge instant) — it never perturbs `v0` and never reorients the arc. The formula F4 thus takes the captured camera-azimuth XZ direction and a fixed `v0`; runtime, preview, and LevelFixture all use the same composition. Any "drag from the thrower" or "thrower kinetic contribution" is a violation and fails closed at fixture validation.

**Burst contact-versus-timeout event time.** Within a fixed substep, the closest-hit SphereCast supplies a normalized contact fraction `α ∈ [0,1]`; `contact_event_time = flight_elapsed + α × dt_max` (`α = 0` is reachable only as the launch-preflight initial-overlap probe before spend — a sweep started already-overlapped reports no hit, so initial overlap cannot occur mid-flight and is never a terminal result; the terminal timeout path never re-runs an overlap probe). The contact-versus-timeout comparison is performed on **integer fixed-tick counts** — `tick_index ≤ timeout_tick`, where `timeout_tick = ⌈3.0 s / dt_max⌉` (exactly 360 at `dt_max = 1/120 s`) — never on the accumulated float `flight_elapsed`, which is derived for diagnostics and replay only; an accumulated-float comparison at exactly tick 360 is float-fragile and forbidden. Collision wins when the contact tick is at or before `timeout_tick`, including exact equality. If the earliest valid contact is later than `timeout_tick`, the timeout boundary wins and the flight resolves at the last simulated sphere center; no late collision, landing, or `NoisePublished` may be emitted. The terminal record carries `terminal_cause ∈ {Collision, Timeout, DeathCancelled}`, `burst_state_after ∈ {Landed, Consumed}`, `terminal_publication_time`, `contact_event_time` (or `null`), `timeout_boundary_s`, `comparison_result`, the winning `tick_index`, and the single winning `flight_handle_id`. `terminal_cause` is the resolution reason and `burst_state_after` is the post-terminal lifecycle state; they are never conflated. Duplicate terminal callbacks are no-ops. Terminal lifecycle staging/cancellation uses a separate `terminal_publication_state` field with values `{not_applicable, pending, published, rejected, invalidated}`. This field belongs only to the terminal lifecycle/transport record: it is not serialized as raw `NoisePublished.fact_publication_state`, does not authorize a gameplay fact, and never expands the raw enum. `DeathCancelled` is `not_applicable` and emits no raw fact; a Collision/Timeout terminal is `pending` until emitter flush, becomes `published` only after a nonzero emitter `fact_id` commits, and becomes `rejected` or `invalidated` on terminal transport failure or lifecycle invalidation. The raw `NoisePublished.fact_publication_state` remains restricted to `{published, rejected, invalidated}` with those published/rejected/invalidated transport semantics. `fact_id` is nullable and is never serialized as numeric zero to mean “not ready.”

**Backlog invariant.** Only completed fixed ticks advance `flight_elapsed`. When accumulated backlog exceeds `max_backlog_ticks × dt_max`, the excess remainder is discarded and serialized as diagnostic/replay data; it is not integrated, does not move the sphere, and does not alter timeout event time. Already-published raw facts and hearing work remain in their own ordered queues. A paused virtual clock accrues no Burst backlog; a resumed render stall is distinct from discarded simulation backlog.

**Reject-newest, fail-closed queues.** Event Bus pending envelopes, the Perception raw-fact queue, deferred boundaries, and Perception hearing work all use the same policy: retain older queued items, retry/backpressure up to the registered retry count, then reject only the newest item if still full. The rejection records `(session_id, attempt_epoch, source_timestamp, fact_id or source_event_id, queue_name, queue_depth, queue_capacity, retry_count, rejection_code)`; it creates no successful relay, decision, or certification pass. Oldest eviction, silent drop, implicit queue stretching, and late-success substitution are forbidden. A rejected item is a fail-closed certification result even when older work later drains successfully.

**Audio lifecycle.** Audio/UI may schedule an emission or guard-proxy cue only from a current-epoch committed source/decision. A cue whose source fact is duplicate, rejected, stale, terminal-cancelled, or from a prior epoch is not scheduled; a queued cue is cancelled at the epoch/death barrier before presentation. A missed virtual-gameplay deadline records `audio-cue-deadline-missed` and never plays late; a duplicate key is ignored; a relay receipt alone never creates guard proxy audio. The only authoritative proxy key is `(session_id, attempt_epoch, fact_id, guard_eid)` after the noise-caused `investigate-commit`; virtual gameplay time remains the onset clock and the true-peak bus limiter remains the level ceiling.

**Saturation.** The immutable corroboration count is capped at three qualifying facts. The first opens the episode; the second and third invoke the profile-specific response. Once the third has been accepted, every later same-area fact until episode resolution is consumed for trace only: it neither re-anchors, resets or extends a timer, recruits another peer, publishes another locked-zone command, allocates an `entry_id`, nor publishes a duplicate escalation. Invalid, stale, out-of-order, out-of-window, out-of-radius, and duplicate facts do not consume corroboration count; they retain their normal keyed outcome.

`noise_response_profile`, queue capacities/retry counts, the event-time terminal policy, and the saturation limit are registry-owned. Runtime, preview, fixture, and replay must consume the same values and record all rejected or uncaptured evidence explicitly.

**Visibility-query contract (2026-09-01, B1 fix).** "The player can see the guard" is a single defined, owned query — not an ambient notion of noticeability. Definition: `visible_to_player(guard) = true` iff a **binary Linecast** from the player's camera-anchored eye point to the guard's **head/upper-body capsule segment** (feet → `guard_eye_height` endpoint, registered occlusion endpoint) is **clear** on the shared E20 `World` mask with `QueryTriggerInteraction.Ignore` — the same mask, endpoint datum, and trigger policy the hearing Linecasts use (OQ2 pins). Owner: the **FSM**, evaluated once per relay-suppression/micro-tell decision tick alongside the suppression evaluation itself (never per frame, never by Audio/UI). Audio/UI consumes the FSM-published boolean and never runs, caches, or re-derives the query. "No LOS" in this document always means exactly this query returning `false`; "cannot see the guard" phrasing is struck in favor of the query's named result. The query is one additional Linecast per suppression decision (bounded by the same per-tick query quota contract, AC-FSM-19).

No persistent "you are loud" ambience, no hearing-cone hum — noise feedback is **event-based**, matching the discrete relay.

**Rustle transition rule.** The controller owns the stride denominator and emits a stable `rustle_id = (session_id, attempt_epoch, stride_id)` with each audio-only rustle. Rustle has two discrete published states: `armed` after positive audible-state displacement begins, and `fired` when the current ledger progress first crosses `rustle_threshold × current_stride_length`; it fires at most once per `stride_id`. Once armed, entering Idle or Crouch does not cancel it, and resuming audible movement does not replay it. A `fired` rustle whose stride never commits (death/reset or other terminal interruption mid-stride) produces no hearing event, no `NoisePublished`, and no phantom ledger commit. Walk↔Run transitions do not reset ledger progress and cannot fire a rustle without positive displacement; if the transition changes the active stride denominator, the current `stride_id` remains stable and the next crossing is evaluated against the latched stride target. No rustle is emitted during Crouch/Idle displacement, and no fired state is replayed on resume. The controller and Audio/UI integration must test state switches immediately before and after the threshold.

| **Hear → Orient** | Perception R5 + FSM (consumes relay) | Guard head snaps / turns toward the **noise position**, not toward the player; if occluded, no turn — the player learns the wall blocked it |
| **Investigate commit** | FSM Investigate | Guard walks to the **area with error** (`r_investigate_error 1.5 m` around the noise point), short look-around sweep, gives up `fruitless` if not reacquired |
| **Hunch telegraph** | FSM + #21 | A hidden-player noise inside a HideSpot pulls a **head-cock/lean**, never a grab — the player reads "he suspects, he doesn't know" |

The FSM/animation path owns the authored motion and rate limit in MVP; #21 does not create the tell or become an MVP dependency |

The hearing relay is **available** to the FSM within `T_hearing ≤ 0.2 s` of the noise commit (B8 — Perception's 5 Hz hearing sub-eval bound). For the certified player-visible reaction path, the consuming FSM decision must publish its orient/commit outcome within `T_fsm ≤ 0.3 s` of relay availability, giving the fairness budget `T_visible = T_hearing + T_fsm ≤ 0.5 s`; this is the acceptance budget, not a claim that every uncaptured runtime tick meets it. The shared FSM clock may still have a general cadence bound `Δt ≤ 0.5 s`, but a route record that cannot demonstrate the dedicated `T_fsm` budget fails its player-visible latency check. Player-local audio is immediate at the gameplay commit; guard proxy audio waits for the authoritative noise-caused `investigate-commit`, so the two cues are intentionally bounded differently and must not be described as frame-identical.

audio evidence. For `audible` rows, `confirmed` remains the only passing onset
state; `unsupported`, `pending`, `estimated`, and `limiter_unsupported` remain
non-passing and are never upgraded by a matrix row. `not_applicable` is valid only
for a declared visual-only or out-of-scope gate and is never counted as a pass.

### Visual — Not owned (explicitly out of scope)

- Default MVP has no emission pulse ring at the noise position. An opt-in, transient accessibility indicator may confirm that a raw noise fact was emitted, but it must not imply that a guard heard it or reveal guard state.
- Residual pips, grade popups, or suspicion meter chrome — owned by Suspicion/Grade GDD.
- Guard vision cone / detection meter fill — owned by Perception GDD.

### Authoring notes

- The merged footstep **voice** must be keyed by the stable `step_id` at the gameplay commit, not by animation step events — the body+accent blend is scheduled from the ledger commit; if any footstep is emitted without that identity, the player hears a non-pip that was not a hearing event (the exact class F5(f)/F5g pins). The `rustle_event` tells remain audio-only and never trigger a hearing event.
- Burst land SFX position must be the **hearing position** (`+ normal × (projectile_radius + ε)`, identical to the position carried in `Noise heard.position`), not the pre-collision arc sample — SFX and hearing share the same point so the audio engine and the gameplay radius are co-located (push-out `normal × 0.05` would put SFX 5 cm outside the wall).

## UI Requirements

Player Noise owns **no persistent HUD**. Hearing readability is delivered through guard behavior, the rustle, and the Suspicion meter (all delegated). The only default UI this system owns is the **world-space Burst pickup/throw prompt**; accessibility feedback below is opt-in, transient, and owned by the Audio/UI integration.

| Element | Trigger | Content | Behavior |
|---------|---------|---------|----------|
| **Burst pickup prompt** | Player enters the shared `F5` pickup predicate — 3D sphere from player feet to pickup anchor plus clear E20 Linecast — for a `Placed` pickup | World-space prompt: key hint (e.g., `[E] Pick up Burst`) + subtle highlight on the placed pickup | Shown only while `Placed` and reachable; hides on leaving the query or on `Carried`; reappears after a leave/re-entry on the next unconsumed pickup. `Interact` evaluates the identical sphere and Linecast, and telemetry fires one `pickup-reached {pickup_id, timestamp, publisher}` on each enter edge. **Pickup emit rule:** pickup is **silent** — no `NoisePublished` publishes on `Placed → Carried`; the only audio is the player-local pickup-confirm SFX. Authors must keep pickup reach clear of patrol routes, guard LOS, and solid geometry so the player is not forced to break a patrol line or collect through a wall |
| **Throw hint** | While `Carried` | Minimal key hint (e.g., `[Q] Throw`) near the reticle or as a control bar item | Visible while exactly one Burst is carried, including while moving, so the affordance is never silently lost. The controller owns readiness: it is enabled only for a stationary resolved `Idle`/standing or successfully resolved `Crouch-Idle` edge; while a `Walk`/`Run` label or nonzero resolved planar speed makes the edge invalid, the hint is visibly disabled and reads `Stop to throw`. The rejected edge preserves `Carried`, emits no gameplay cue/fact, and shows one transient local `Throw unavailable while moving` notice; accessibility mode may add the private `burst-launch-blocked` visual/haptic cue. | **Full-room restart** | Both authored Burst pickups are `Consumed` (post-double-spend) or player selects the recovery action | Discoverable pause-menu **Restart Room** entry; after both spends, a contextual prompt points to the same action | One explicit recovery boundary runs the atomic `full_room_restart_transaction`: clears carried slot, restores both pickups to `Placed`, resets gate markers, discards prior-epoch work, and starts a fresh session envelope with `attempt_epoch=1`. It never silently refunds a single spend during an active attempt. |
| **No default HUD element** | Always | No noise meter, no persistent "you are loud" glow, no default emission ring | Silence is a choice the player feels through consequence, not a bar they watch (anti-puppeting + Pillar 2). Accessibility exceptions are transient and do not expose guard state. **Emission-feedback design decision (Signal A, 2026-08-30):** the canonical emission feedback is the **guard orient tell** (a guard's head-cock or pause-on-alert when a noise relay reaches it, fired only after the noise-caused `investigate-commit`, per AC19). The player learns the noise contract by observing which actions the facility reacts to; the radii `R_walk / R_run / R_burst` and the corroboration window are **discovered through play**, not surfaced as HUD readouts. AC16's transient visual emission flash remains the accessibility-only path. This is an intentional "system-readable through consequence" choice that preserves the quiet-architect fantasy and the Pillar-2 audit trail (cadence monotonicity, see F3(3)) without converting the noise contract into a meter the player watches. **Out of scope / delegated:** Suspicion meter / residual sink marker (Suspicion/Grade); guard vision cone rendering (Perception / Guard AI); inventory chrome — there is no inventory; one carry max is a world-state, not a bag.

**Accessibility / input notes:** prompts must be readable at the authored reach radius under URP; contrast must not rely on the pickup's emissive alone; all prompts are mouse/keyboard navigable; no hold-to-pickup in MVP (reach + explicit `Interact` edge keeps the loop tight). **Audio accessibility:** in accessibility mode, exactly one **transient visual emission flash** accompanies each raw `NoisePublished`, keyed by `fact_id` and colored by movement/Burst tier; it confirms emission only, never that a guard heard it. A haptic pulse and time-bounded subtitle-style cue mirror each rustle/footstep emission when enabled. When a guard actually consumes a relay, Audio/UI emits at most one coarse directional reception cue per `(fact_id, guard_eid)` internally, coalesced for presentation; blocked or out-of-range guards emit zero reception cues. These cues never expose `guard_eid`, exact guard count, or world position. The accessibility mode is an alternative feedback path, not a gameplay channel or default HUD.
